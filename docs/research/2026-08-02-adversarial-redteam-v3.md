# Adversarial Red-Team v3 — the Trainer as a Training System — 2026-08-02

A re-attack (the ADVERSARIAL leg of `docs/BACKLOG.md`), the first since the app shipped
**RV4** (wall-clock Leitner spacing + retention analytics), **RV5** (jittered distraction),
and the experiential tier **ET7** (dealer-pace bursts), **ET3** (bet/sit/leave), **ET5**
(fatigue drift), and the flagship **ET1** (downswing / tilt inoculation). Those closed most
of the v2 (2026-07-28) list. This pass does **not** re-report shipped or already-known-open
items (RV6 integrated score, RV7 wong-miss-on-deal-in, RV8 cover, RV9 final-RC-only count
grading are all still live in BACKLOG and re-confirmed, not re-litigated here; RV2 rounding
and RV3 Fab-4 are settled/out-of-scope). Instead it attacks the **newly shipped code** and
looks for the next tier of gaps — with emphasis on *how well* the ET/RV features actually
train the thing they claim to.

Two personas: **[PRO]** ("what still fails at a real table for someone who aces every drill
here?") and **[SCI]** ("what does this regime mistrain, over-train, or leave unmeasured?").
Every claim is grounded in cited code, tagged **[FACT]** (verified in source) or
**[INFERENCE]**.

**Where the new code is genuinely strong (verified, NOT gaps):** RV4 is fully wired, not a
stub — `srWeight` feeds both draw paths (`flashcards.ts:122`, `deviationQuiz.ts:349`) and
`reviewCard`/`isGapReview` drive the grade path and a real retained-vs-in-drill series
(`gradeAnswer.ts:226,263`); the Leitner lapse-accounting only counts forgets past
`LEARNED_BOX` (`spacedRepetition.ts:81`), which is honest. RV5's jitter is real
(seeded per-window burst position). ET3's grading rule is faithfully transcribed from the
researched consensus (`betSitLeave.ts:41-45` matches `2026-08-01-...§7`). These are good.

| 3 | PRO+SCI | **ET3's scenario generator draws true count and decks-remaining INDEPENDENTLY, so it presents physically-impossible snapshots and makes depth a pure distractor on positive counts — and it never trains the back-counter's wong-IN entry decision the consensus centers.** | **[FACT]** `betSitLeave.ts:69-71` draws `trueCount` (−4..3) and `decksRemaining` (0.5..6.0) from independent RNG calls with no coupling. ## Top 3 to build next

1. **Fix the flagship (ET1) — findings #1 + #7.** The downswing session is the marquee
   experiential feature and it currently under-delivers on every axis: the pressure is
   removed (count shown), the grade can't be failed, the realism is a loss-wall, the chips
   don't match the profile, and nothing is persisted. Hiding the count, deriving chips from
   the spread, interspersing real wins, and writing a Stats series would turn it from a
   demo into the tilt-inoculation trainer it was scoped to be. Highest impact because the
   feature already exists — this is quality, not new ground.

2. **An integrated "produce a true count" drill (#2).** The single biggest *skill* gap a
   pro would name: the app drills RC, depth, and division separately but never the live act
   of generating a TC under load. Distinct from RV6 and cheap-ish (composes over the count
   drill + `trueCount()` + a tolerance band). This is the counting-subsystem analogue of
   RV6's play-level integration and arguably more fundamental.

3. **Pedagogical sequencing + gating in the picker (#4).** The app preaches competence-gated
   difficulty and then hands beginners a flat menu where Downswing sits next to Count Drill.
   Grouping + soft-gating the pressure modes behind the existing `computeUnlockedTier`
   signal is low effort and stops the app from mistraining via premature pressure — and it
   makes every ET/RV feature discoverable in the intended order.

Honorable mentions: couple ET3's scenario generator so it stops teaching impossible boards
(#3, S–M, and it also unlocks the untrained wong-in decision); add latency-drift to ET5 (#5)
since slowing-down is the earlier fatigue tell; and give RV4 a "due today" surface (#6) so
the spacing engine becomes visible and actionable instead of silent.
