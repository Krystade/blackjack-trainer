# Adversarial Red-Team v2 — the Trainer as a Training System — 2026-07-28

A re-attack, the ADVERSARIAL leg of the standing workflow (`docs/BACKLOG.md`). Since the
last red-team (`2026-07-23-adversarial-redteam.md`) the app shipped R1–R9 + D1: decision-
latency telemetry, accuracy-gated difficulty, Leitner-lite miss-weighting, distraction
training, interleaved "Mixed" mode, wong-out / Sit Out, count-peek accountability, count-
bias + pair-cancellation drills, messy cards, and the discard-tray depth cue. Those closed
most of the 2026-07-23 list. This pass looks for the **next tier** of gaps and does **not**
re-report shipped items — where it touches a shipped feature it critiques the *implementation*.

Two personas: **[PRO]** a professional counter ("what still fails at a real table for
someone who aces every drill here?") and **[SCI]** a learning scientist ("what does this
regime mistrain, over-train, or leave unmeasured?"). Every claim is grounded in the cited
code and tagged **[FACT]** (verified in source) or **[INFERENCE]**. Nothing here is argued
from an imagined feature.

Where the app is now genuinely strong (verified, NOT gaps): the deviation engine is honest
about rulesets (H17/S17 index sets, `deviations.ts:40-75`), the grader has a real
missed/phantom-deviation taxonomy (`grade.ts:47-124`), latency is captured on graded events
(`grade.ts:32`), and the wong-out engine keeps the count/penetration running through a
sit-out round (`game.ts:445-496`) — that's an engine-honest feature, not a drill hack.

| 1 | PRO+SCI | **No bankroll / variance / risk-of-ruin training, and no emotional-downswing inoculation.** The app can grade a perfect bet ramp but never makes the learner *survive a losing shoe* or reason about how much to risk. | **[FACT]** `riskOfRuin` is a manually-typed CVCX display field only (`store/types.ts:174`, entered in `ProfileEditor.tsx:612`, shown static in `Stats.tsx:226`) — nothing computes it. `bankrollDelta` is a per-session tally (`types.ts:234`) off `bankrollStart` default 100 (`types.ts:136`); there is no multi-session bankroll journey, no equity curve, no Kelly/N0, no drawdown surfacing. Bets are graded `bet === expectedUnits` (`game.ts:371`) irrespective of bankroll. | Ruin and tilt — not skill — are the dominant real-world reason counters bust. A learner who aces every drill has never felt a 40-unit downswing, never sized a unit off a real bankroll, and has no intuition for the variance that makes flat-betting the count feel "wrong" mid-storm. The app trains the edge and ignores the risk that governs whether you can deploy it. | A "bankroll run" mode: play/auto-sim N shoes at the configured spread, render the equity curve + max drawdown + a computed RoR vs starting bankroll; optionally a hold-your-spread prompt during a losing streak. | M–L |
| 2 | SCI+PRO | **The app asserts ONE true-count rounding convention (floor toward −∞) as the single correct answer and grades everything on it — including at index boundaries where the convention flips the decision.** | **[FACT]** `count.ts:24` floors toward −∞; `insuranceCorrect` is `tc >= 3` (`strategy.ts:26`); the TC drill grades exact-match to the floored value (`trueCountDrill.ts:39`) and the table TC check grades `tcGuess === actualTc` (`game.ts:635`). **[FACT]** the *same app* grades deck estimation within a ±0.5-deck band (`deckEstimation.ts:63`), acknowledging depth is an estimate. **[INFERENCE]** RC +5 at 2 decks = TC +2.5 → app = +2 (decline insurance); a learner taught round-to-nearest (a legitimate, widely-taught method) says +3 (take) and is graded WRONG at the single most count-sensitive call. | This mistrains twice: it punishes a correct alternative method, and it pretends the TC has integer precision the real quantity (RC ÷ an *estimated* depth) never has. Near an index the EV difference is ~flat, yet the app scores it pass/fail on a coin-flip of convention. Someone drilled to exact-floor here will freeze or mis-call at a real table where their depth read is ±half a deck. | Grade TC checks / deviation lookups within a tolerance band around index boundaries (or let the profile pick a rounding convention and grade consistently); at minimum flag near-index calls as EV-flat rather than binary-wrong. | S–M |
| 3 | PRO | **No surrender (Fab 4) deviations exist — deviation content is hit/stand/double/split only.** | **[FACT]** the entire deviation set (`deviations.ts:40-59` H17, `:64-75` S17) is `stand`/`hit`/`double`/`split`; there is no `surrender`-action deviation anywhere, and the grader's phantom-deviation scan explicitly only handles `hard`/`pair10` kinds (`grade.ts:106-120`). A learner who aces every deviation drill here has trained zero count-based surrender decisions — a whole class of real-table calls the app can't even represent or grade. | Add the Fab 4 as `surrender`-kind deviations (gated on `canSurrender`), extend the phantom-deviation scan + deviation quiz to cover them. | S–M |
| 4 | SCI | **R3 is error-weighting, not spacing — there is no time-based scheduling and no cross-session retention loop.** | **[FACT]** the shipped weighting is `missWeight = 1 + 2·missCount` (BACKLOG R3 / `weightedDraw.ts`) — a function of error *count* with no time-since-last-seen term; nothing schedules review by elapsed days and nothing re-tests after a delay to measure retained vs in-drill accuracy. | The spacing effect (the strongest, most-cited result in the training-science leg) is fundamentally *temporal*. The app built the miss-weighting half and skipped the spacing half. Between casino trips — exactly when indices and chart cells decay — nothing schedules a review and nothing measures whether the skill survived. In-drill accuracy therefore systematically overstates table-day competence. | Add a last-seen timestamp per cell/index + a Leitner box promoted by *time*; a session-open "cold review" of items unseen for N days that logs retained-accuracy as its own series. | M |
| 5 | SCI | **Distraction interruptions fire on a fixed, fully predictable cadence.** | **[FACT]** `isDistractionPoint` triggers deterministically every 7th (occasional) / 3rd (relentless) card, `(shownIndex+1) % interval === 0`, no jitter (`distraction.ts:104-120`). | Divided-attention training works *because* the interruption is unanticipated — you must protect the count continuously. A fixed cadence lets the learner pre-buffer the count right before each known interruption, converting a dual-task drill into a scheduled single-task one. It inflates count-kept scores without training the real skill (holding a count through a *surprise*). | Seed-driven randomized inter-distraction gap (e.g. geometric around the target mean) so timing can't be predicted; keep the mean tied to the freq setting. | XS |
| 6 | PRO+SCI | **The table never scores integrated, simultaneous competence — the three skills are graded in separate silos and counting on the table stays optional.** | **[FACT]** bet, play, insurance, and count-check events are graded independently (`game.ts:371/512/620`); the TC peek remains available on the table (R7 logs it but never *requires* unaided counting), and there is no composite score combining count + play + bet accuracy under time. | The terminal real-world task is doing all of it at once, under dealer-speed load. The app trains and measures the *parts* superbly but never the *integration*, so a green dashboard on the parts can't certify table-readiness — the exact plateau trap the last red-team flagged, now shifted up a level from "no latency" to "no integrated assessment." | An "assessed table run" mode: peek disabled, count checks sampled, latency captured, emitting ONE integrated table-ready score with a pass bar (reuses R1 latency + existing graders). | M |
| 7 | PRO | **R5 rewards dealing in at a should-wong count as a "correct bet"; the sit-out decision is only graded when the player actually sits out.** | **[FACT]** `startRound` grades `bet === expectedUnits` (`game.ts:371`); the `wong` event fires *only* from `sitOut()` (`game.ts:445-453`). Min-betting through deep negative counts scores "correct bet" every round and never surfaces a wong-miss. (This is the R5 FOLLOW-ON deferred in BACKLOG — re-flagged as a live *mistraining*, not just a missing feature.) | The whole point of R5 was training the exit decision; instrumenting only the sit-out half means the learner is positively reinforced for the wrong choice (playing the minimum when they should be back-counting), which is worse than silence. | When `betSpreadOn` and `sitOutIsCorrect(preDealTc)` but the player deals in, push a `wong`-miss event alongside the bet grade (audit the event-order-sensitive tests first, per BACKLOG). | S |
| 8 | PRO | **Bet grading still demands exact ramp conformity and there is no cover/camouflage concept — the app anti-trains the #1 surveillance tell.** | **[FACT]** `bet === expectedUnits` exact match every round (`game.ts:371`); no cover mode, no flag for raising-off-the-top or exact-step laddering. The app rewards the exact behavior surveillance flags, hundreds of reps deep, with no counter-signal. | Optional cover mode: grade bets within ±1 ramp step + flag tell patterns; a short cover/heat explainer (judgment content, not fully drillable). | S |
| 9 | SCI | **Count-drill grading still checks only the final RC, so offsetting errors score as perfect and errors aren't localized.** | **[FACT]** the count check compares a single number (`game.ts:618`, `rc === actualRc`); a +1 slip and a −1 slip in one run net to "correct," and a wrong run can't say where the count was lost. ## Top 3 to consider next

1. **Bankroll / variance / RoR training (#1).** The single biggest *category* the app has never
   touched — it trains the edge and entirely omits the risk management and emotional inoculation
   that decide whether a counter survives long enough to realize that edge. Highest new-ground
   value; M–L but nothing about it is a drill-shaped patch, it's a genuine missing pillar.
2. **Rounding-convention honesty + tolerance grading (#2).** Cheapest fix that stops an *active
   mistraining*: the app currently marks a legitimate method wrong at the most count-sensitive
   call and pretends the TC is exact. Also unblocks the deferred R6 "tolerant count-check" half.
   S–M, high correctness return.
3. **Time-based spacing + retention measurement (#4).** The training-science leg's headline
   result is spacing, and R3 shipped only the error-weighting half. Adding a temporal Leitner box
   + a cold-review retained-accuracy series is the difference between measuring in-drill
   performance and measuring the thing that actually predicts a good casino trip. M.

Near-free honorable mention: randomize the distraction cadence (#5) — a few lines that convert a
predictable, gameable drill into genuine divided-attention training.
