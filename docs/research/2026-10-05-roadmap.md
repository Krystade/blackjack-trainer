## (a) Status per goal

### G1. Phone alone, loud speaker + voice answers
- **Proven (phone-measured, desk, iOS 18.7, installed app):**
  - Web Audio decoded-buffer playback with the mic open: loud speaker 7/7 (668a6a5 blind check, plus the
    08:11 run with the ring switch off). `<audio>` element: 2/7, 0/3 on the cleanest run.
  - Ring switch on silent does not silence Web Audio while the mic is open (08:09 run).
  - Call Audio Routing = Speaker does not help (ruled out). `audioSession.type='playback'`, close-mic-then-speak
    and "Switch" mode are all disproved (ec67e35, 0372c09).
  - Shipped: clips use Web Audio while a voice session runs (8195254), and stay on it for the rest of the page
    once the mic has been open (7826a3e, after Jack's log showed the element dropping to the earpiece).
- **Assumed:**
  - That a whole real drill (not the 6-play Speaker check) stays on the speaker end to end. No
    `clip-chain path=webaudio` log from a live drill has been read yet.
  - That decoded-buffer audio sounds fine. "They sounded identical" is one listener, one sitting. The caa5a73
    stutter was the MediaElementSource route, a different path.
  - That the new "your turn"/"got it" tones are on the speaker. They are generated by Web Audio, so likely.
- **Unknown:**
  - Live-TTS fallback lines (`speechSynthesis`) and push-to-talk windows (mic closed between presses) are not on
    the Web Audio path (docs/TODO.md "Not covered"). Whether they hit the earpiece is unmeasured.
  - Ring switch ON with the mic closed made Web Audio silent twice. The code uses Web Audio only while a voice
    session runs, which avoids it, but the deafened/idle gaps are untested with the switch on.
  - The "fresh page after reload plays the element on the earpiece" oddity (never explained). Low value now.
- **Verdict:** close to done. One real drill on the phone confirms it.

### G2. Bluetooth: car speakers + voice answers
- **Proven (drives 09-27, 09-29):** clips reach the car speakers over Bluetooth; wheel skip-forward/back arrive as
  nexttrack/previoustrack, also with the screen off; opening the mic drops the car onto the hands-free call route
  (the wheel then belongs to the "call"); the output route recovers within about a second of the mic closing
  (drive-findings sections 2 and 3).
- **Assumed:**
  - That Web Audio with the mic open ALSO reaches the car speakers over HFP. It was only ever measured with the
    element, and 8195254 changed the path. Plausible, never heard. This is the biggest regression risk in the
    whole tree, and it is cheap to check.
  - That a drill works with the wheel as the "yes" while the mic is closed (the 2026-09-11 design). With voice on
    in the car the wheel is spoken for.
- **Unknown:**
  - WHICH mic the recogniser is on with Bluetooth connected (car HFP vs iPhone mic). The log has seen both across
    runs (drive-findings 4.4), and the spectral probe `highRatio` (a4d87bb) was never run on the freeway. The
    "Bluetooth: phone mic?" kit exists to answer this directly.
  - Whether HFP in this Corolla is wideband (mSBC 16kHz) or narrowband (8kHz). Decides if the car mic is a ceiling
    or an upgrade (TODO G2-b).
  - Whether the car's "hands-free mic" setting or iOS device type changes the input (G2-e, no code, never tried).

### G3. Recognition at freeway noise
- **Proven:** the quiet-room failure is the model, not noise (the "Add" misrecognition at 0.737). The two-word
  forms and taught aliases fixed the quiet room: 10/10 on one-word and two-word alike, round two of the desk
  calibration (TODO results table).
- **Assumed:**
  - That road noise is where accuracy collapses. Nobody has measured it. No calibration has ever run above
    parked-quiet. The whole recognition investment (bd060dd, 18cb059, the 10-alternatives request) is unscored in
    motion.
  - That `offered>1` can happen. It was 1 of 1 in 5 of 5 samples, so alternatives probably do not help
    (TODO G3-c).
  - That the new turn-taking (70b12d6: deaf until real speech end, 4s margin, tones) removes the app-hears-itself
    loop. It reproduced from a real log and the voice-table spec caught the 'got it' swallow, but no drive log
    confirms it yet.
- **Unknown:**
  - Freeway accuracy, one-word vs two-word, through the car mic vs the phone mic. This is THE open measurement.
  - First-mic-open route change that kills capture for about 20s (desk, 10-05). Reproduction rate and whether it
    also hits drill answers is unknown. Drills could lose their first answer(s) of every page load.
  - "But" was fuzzy-matched to "split" (found 10-05, fixed in the commits above). The matcher's false-accept
    behaviour under noise (random road words mapped to commands) is unmeasured. At freeway noise false accepts may
    matter more than misses, because a wrong answer is a recorded miss.
- **Verdict:** the largest unknown, and the one that justifies the next drive.

### G4. UX/UI

- **Unknown:** whether a car-mode screen (big text, one status line, no toggles) is wanted. Not built (TODO G4).

## (d) Risks and things to stop doing

**Stop doing**
1. Re-running element-vs-Web-Audio, Call Audio Routing, reload and "which speaker with BT" experiments on the
   desk. Settled (7/7, ruled out). Strip the `<audio>` element rows from car kits.
2. Native-app or Capacitor proposals. Closed by Jack (TODO G1-c). Do not re-raise unless he does.
3. Debugging in the car, and any multi-experiment drive. The 2026-10-01 drive card exists because a drive was
   spent re-asking 40 answered questions. Two kits per drive, each with a stated decision, is the ceiling.
4. Shipping recognition changes without a labelled measurement. bd060dd, 18cb059 and the 10-alternatives ask
   shipped and none has a road number. Nothing more goes in until D1/D2 exists.
5. Cull/rename passes. The Settings and wording passes are done. Further tidy-ups should come from something
   Jack hit, not from inspection.
6. Updating TODO after the fact. Stale items are what made the goal table say "G1 blocked" after G1 was largely
   fixed.
7. Treating a one-listener "sounded identical" as proof. Ask Jack to listen at the drive for stutter in the
   first long clip once.

**Risks**
- **G2 regression from 8195254:** Web Audio might not reach the car speakers over HFP. Gate with P1. Mitigation:
  keep the element path selectable when the route has a Bluetooth output (the app already logs routes).
- **The car mic may be narrowband.** If P2 says so, voice over BT hits a hard ceiling no web code can lift. The
  fallback is phone speaker for sound in motion, or wheel buttons for yes/no where the drills allow.
- **Calibration under noise measures mic-open reliability, not just words.** If `nothing-heard` dominates, the
  accuracy number is meaningless. Read the verdict breakdown (right / wrong-action / not-a-command / nothing-heard)
  before concluding anything about the model.
- **iOS changes silently.** The route behaviour is on iOS 18.7. Pin the build, and note the iOS version in each
  logged result.
- **Bundle/latency risk for Vosk-class recogniser (about 40MB model, and it still opens a page mic so G1's
  earpiece issue applies, with Web Audio as the only escape).** Only start 4b if D1/D2 are bad.
- **Operator risk:** he is driving. Everything on the road must work by voice and by wheel with no taps. Check
  the kit's driving steps actually have that property before the drive, or restrict D1/D2 to a passenger or a
  parked/idling run (lower value, label it).
- **One operator, one phone, one car.** Do not over-generalise a result that has n=1 per condition. Two drives
  agreeing is the bar for changing a default.
